# Dan Brown — Five Recurring Themes in Product IA

Recurring structural forces that appear in complex (especially enterprise) digital products.

## 1. Status
Many products attach status labels to important objects (Draft, Active, Archived, Pending, etc.).  
Status creates complexity because reality is messy. Design for clear status models and transitions.

## 2. Containers
Products create containers that hold other things (Workspaces, Projects, Folders, Collections…).  
Decisions about containers made early tend to reverberate for years. Choose container models carefully.

## 3. Rules
Products encode process rules (who can do what, under which conditions, in which sequence).  
A product cannot and should not assume responsibility for every aspect of a real-world process. Draw clear boundaries.

## 4. Time
Objects have lifecycles. Users need to understand past, present, and future states.  
Humans are generally better at interpreting temporal complexity than rigid computer models. Design accordingly.

## 5. People
Enterprise and multi-user products involve collaboration, ownership, assignment, and decision-making across people.  
Structure must support shared understanding and clear responsibility.

These themes are not present in every product, but most complex products deal with at least one of them significantly.
